Proposal · 25 November 2026

Close Moodle from 5 pm to 1 am until the new year

We are replacing the platform the Moodle learning site runs on. Each step of that work needs the site closed. Usage data shows almost nobody uses Moodle in the evening, so a standing evening closure would let the work go ahead as soon as each step is ready, with almost no one affected.

What the numbers show

  • Daytime is busy. Between 8 am and 4 pm on a weekday, 13 to 36 people use the site in an average hour. The busiest single hour had 227.
  • Evenings are nearly empty. About 1 person uses the site between 5 and 6 pm, and fewer than 1 an hour after that. The whole 5 pm to 1 am period adds up to about 3 person-hours of use on a typical weekday.
  • No evening had more than 4 people in any hour across eight weeks. Most evenings, one or two people visit briefly.
  • Weekends are quiet all day, with at most 2 people in any hour.
  • The course-development (test) site is quieter still: 251 recorded actions in eight weeks, never more than 2 people in an hour, and one person, once, after 5 pm.

Why an evening closure helps

  • The work moves faster. The replacement happens in steps: moving the test site, then the learning site, and moving back if anything goes wrong. With a standing closure, each step can start at 5 pm on the first evening it is ready, without arranging and announcing a separate outage.
  • The work is safer. Each step has hours instead of minutes, so nothing is rushed. Nobody is in the middle of a quiz or an upload when the site changes.
  • Users get a predictable schedule. The same hours every evening are easier to plan around than occasional unscheduled outages.
  • It addresses a real risk. The current database needed an 8-hour manual recovery in May 2026. The replacement removes that design, and the sooner it is finished the sooner that risk is gone.

What users will see

Moodle's standard maintenance page, saying the site reopens at 1 am. Anything saved before 5 pm is kept. Background work, such as reporting course completions to ELM, resumes when the site reopens.

Before the first closure

  1. Tell users with a banner on the site and an email a few days ahead.
  2. Check that no quiz or assignment closes between 5 pm and 1 am before the new year, and move any that do.
  3. Confirm that completions reaching ELM a few hours later than usual is acceptable.
  4. Agree who can cancel the closure for a particular evening if someone needs the site.

After January: a permanent evening window?

The same pattern would make a permanent nightly maintenance window inexpensive: platform updates, security patches and backups could all happen without touching the working day. Before proposing that, we would check:

  • a full year of usage, because mandatory-training deadlines and the fiscal year-end may look different from October and November;
  • whether any evening users, such as shift workers, have a real need for the site at those hours;
  • what the business owner and learners expect from the service.

The query behind this page can cover the past year with no extra effort.

The ask

Approve closing the Moodle learning site and the course-development site every day from 5 pm to 1 am Pacific, starting once approved and ending on 31 December 2026.

Source. Moodle's own activity log on the production site: every action by a signed-in person through the website or its apps, counted by hour in Pacific time, 1 October to 24 November 2026 (39 weekdays and 16 weekend days, 147,696 actions). "People" means different signed-in accounts active in that hour. The test site covers the eight weeks before its maintenance period began on 18 November.
